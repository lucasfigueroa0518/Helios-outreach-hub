# Subject lines

This block governs only the `subject` field. Body prose still follows the first-contact skill. The opening sentence must fulfill the subject; otherwise do not change body craft because of this block.

## Governing principle

A strong first-contact subject should look like a legitimate internal business conversation, not an advertisement. Its job is not to summarize Helios, pitch an offer, or manufacture maximum curiosity. Its job is to make the recipient think: this appears relevant to something I care about.

Prefer topics that earn a qualified reply over empty open-bait.

## Helios topic lean

When several prospect-world anchors are available, prefer ones adjacent to Helios AI, automation, and intelligent operations (lead response, conversion, booking or form friction, content systems, retention or review volume, operational cadence) over generic marketing-agency framing (brand polish, social media management as such, vague “growth”).

Never put that lean into the subject as product language. Name their world, not our stack.

## Rules

1. Speak about their world, not Helios.
   Anchor to a recognizable priority, problem, initiative, market, location, customer group, or responsibility that belongs to the prospect.
   Good: Dealer lead response / Miami leasing demand / Customer retention / Website conversion
   Bad: Helios Marketing Services / Our AI-powered platform / Grow with Helios / Social media management proposal

2. Keep it brutally short.
   Default to one to four words. Treat about 40 characters as a practical ceiling, not a target. A short company or place qualifier is acceptable when it adds real specificity (Website conversion at Acme). Do not write a sentence-length subject.

3. Be specific rather than clever.
   Name the category of conversation. Do not use hollow curiosity or check-in language: Quick question, An idea, Thoughts?, Opportunity, Checking in.
   Stronger: Service appointment leads / South Florida expansion / Abandoned form leads / Customer review volume

4. Personalize with context, not mail-merge theater.
   When the snapshot includes a company or person, default to naming them in the subject if it fits in the length budget — usually the company as a topic qualifier (Allen Tate lead response, lead response at Paracle). Use the person's name only when it clearly identifies the thread (Maria — Carolinas listing volume) — never as a lone salutation (Lucas, …) or empty check-in (Thoughts, Maria?). This is structural specificity, not mail-merge theater.
   Also prefer a current initiative, location, launch or expansion, visible friction, language the prospect uses publicly, or a role responsibility when choosing the topic.
   Do not rely on first-name-only salutations, hollow check-ins, or sender+company mashups: "Lucas, quick question" / "Question for Acme" / "Acme + Helios" / "Thoughts, Lucas?"

5. Do not sell in the subject.
   No claims, features, discounts, ROI promises, meeting asks, superlatives, marketing buzzwords, or company descriptions.
   Avoid: Increase leads by 40% / Free marketing audit / Best Miami agency / AI-powered lead generation / 15-minute meeting?

6. Relevance before curiosity.
   Curiosity only when attached to something recognizable (Your booking flow — not Noticed something; Sunset weekday traffic — not One thought).
   Test: could the prospect see why the topic might matter without knowing who sent it?

7. Sound like a human colleague.
   Plain internal language: Lead response times / Website inquiries / Brickell customer traffic.
   Not marketing-speak: Unlock explosive growth / Transform your digital presence / Personalized solution for Acme.

8. Never manufacture familiarity.
   No Re:, Fwd:, Following up, Our conversation, or a mutual connection who did not introduce you. Do not imply an existing relationship that the snapshot does not support.
   Exception: when relationship geometry is a real warm introduction, the introducer's name or the shared context may appear; still keep the subject short and non-deceptive.

9. Punctuation sparingly.
   No exclamation marks, all caps, decorative symbols, emoji, or unnecessary question marks. Default to none.

10. Subject and opening work as a pair.
    The subject names the topic. The first body sentence supplies the evidence or reason. Do not open with greeting-card filler or a Helios autobiography that ignores the subject.
    Fulfill the promise immediately: if the subject is Dealer lead response, the opening must be about that friction — not a Helios capabilities paragraph.

## Output shape

- One plain subject string. No "Subject:" label, no variants, no line breaks.
- Lowercase or sentence case as a colleague would type; avoid Title Case Benefit Sheen.
